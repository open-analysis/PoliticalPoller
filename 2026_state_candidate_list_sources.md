# 2026 Candidate-List Sources for All 50 States: Scraper Configuration Guide

Most states run a state-level HTML candidate list: 32 of 50 states have one that can be scraped like Minnesota's. Sixteen publish only PDF or XLSX documents, and two (Texas and Ohio) are truly county-decentralized. As of October 1, 2026, filing has closed for the November 3 general election in every state. Every list is therefore complete and should be scraped now as general-election lists. The exceptions are a few special elections, such as Louisiana's December 12 special, which has qualifying on Oct 14–16, 2026.

## TL;DR
- **32 live HTML / 16 static document / 2 decentralized / 0 aggregator-only / 0 none.** Every state publishes some official list, so you never need to fall back on Ballotpedia. The catch is that many state lists cover only federal, statewide, legislative and judicial offices. County and municipal races usually sit with county officials, except where the state hosts them (MN, WA, KY, OK).
- **Start with Washington, Oklahoma, Kentucky, Florida, Missouri, Illinois and Alaska.** They have stable, parameterized URLs that return HTML candidate tables, and several include county races. Several Western/Plains states (MT, ND, SD, NM, WA) use a near-identical `CandidateList.aspx?e(id)=` URL pattern, so one reusable parser should cover them. Their likely common vendor is BPro Inc. (Pierre, SD), maker of TotalVote, which KNOWiNK acquired in 2020. BPro's Microsoft Marketplace listing says its "Election Night Reporting technology is currently used statewide in Montana, Nebraska, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oregon, and South Dakota" and was set to go live statewide in Washington in 2019. That confirms the vendor relationship, but not that the vendor built the candidate-list module.
- **Only Minnesota is known to have bot protection.** No other state's bot protection could be determined from search results, so treat it as unknown until you test each one live. ASP.NET WebForms sites (KY, IL, OR, MT, ND, SD, NM, WA, NV) may need ViewState/postback handling even without a CAPTCHA.

## Key Findings

- **Coverage scope matters more than format.** A "live database" state often holds only state-filed offices. Florida's `CanList.asp` covers federal, statewide and legislative races, but county races are with the 67 county Supervisors of Elections. North Carolina's state PDFs come in a "State only view" and a "Full county view". Kentucky is the model hybrid: a statewide search plus a separate "Candidate Filings with the County Clerk" search, both on web.sos.ky.gov.
- **Several states expose election IDs in URLs.** Examples: Washington `?e=898` (primary) and `?e=899` (general), Florida `?elecid=20261103-GEN`, Kentucky `?id=27`, South Dakota `?eid=773/774`, Montana `?e=450002928`, Alaska `?election=26prim`. That makes per-cycle configuration a matter of changing one parameter.
- **Some static-document states rename their files with every update.** In Iowa, New Hampshire, Tennessee and Wisconsin the filenames carry dates or version suffixes (e.g., "as of 3-10-2026", "cumulative-filings-6.29.26", "_1.pdf"). Scrape the landing page and follow its links instead of hard-coding the PDF URL.
- **Scale benchmarks:** Washington's primary list shows 602 offices and 1,108 candidates, and its general list 599 offices and 887 candidates. On April 3, 2026, the Oklahoma State Election Board (Secretary Paul Ziriax) said "a total of 611 candidates filed for state, federal, legislative, or judicial office," the second-largest filing period in 25 years, behind 794 in 2018. County candidates file with county election boards and are not in that count. Arkansas Money & Politics, citing Secretary of State Cole Jester's office, reported that "106 Republicans, 39 Democrats and 122 non-partisan candidates had filed as of 5 p.m." on opening day, Nov 3, 2025.

## Recommendations

**Tier 1: build first.** These are live HTML lists with parameterized URLs, and none has a known CAPTCHA (to be confirmed by testing).
1. **Washington**: one URL per election (`e=`), with a county filter (`c=`) and local races included.
2. **Oklahoma**: a REST-style path holding the date range and county code; includes county offices.
3. **Kentucky**: separate statewide and county-clerk searches, so you get near-complete coverage from one domain.
4. **Florida**: `CanList.asp?elecid=`. A clean table with qualification status and primary/general outcomes.
5. **Missouri, Illinois, Alaska, Maryland, Virginia, Delaware**: single-page or few-page HTML lists. Maryland, Virginia and Delaware also host county/local lists or Excel exports.
6. **Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, New Mexico**: they share the `CandidateList.aspx?eid=` pattern. Build one parser and test it across all four. The likely common vendor is BPro Inc. (TotalVote, now part of KNOWiNK): its Marketplace listing names Montana, New Mexico, North Dakota and South Dakota as statewide users of its election technology, but it does not specifically name the candidate-list module.

**Tier 2: live but more work.** Oregon (ORESTAR search form), Pennsylvania, Arizona, Arkansas, Georgia, Idaho, Kansas, Louisiana, South Carolina, Rhode Island, Nevada, Hawaii, Michigan, Utah, Massachusetts, Mississippi and Vermont. These likely involve search forms, postbacks or JavaScript-rendered tables. **Minnesota** goes last because its CAPTCHA will need a headless browser, throttling or a manual export.

**Tier 3: document parsing.** CA, CO, IA, NC, NJ, WI, WY, NE, NH, TN, AL and ME. Use a PDF table extractor (e.g., pdfplumber or camelot), or read the XLSX directly for Maine (and Delaware/Maryland exports). Scrape the landing pages to find the current filename. For NC, the "Full county view" PDF already includes county contests, so no per-county work is needed.

**Tier 4: per-county configuration.** Texas (254 counties plus party filings) and Ohio (88 county BOEs posting PDFs). Also consider per-county work anywhere you need local races the state doesn't host: CA (58 ROVs), FL (67 SOEs), NC, IL (e.g., Chicago BOE), MI (county clerks). Prioritize the most populous counties.

**Verify before coding:** CT, IN, NY and WV, where the exact list URL or format wasn't confirmed. Also check deadlines marked "not verified".

## Caveats
- Bot protection was not testable through search. "Unknown" does not mean "unprotected". Many state sites sit behind Cloudflare or Akamai WAFs that may challenge non-browser clients.
- Some URLs (KS, NM, ND, RI and the Hawaii report) were seen only as citations in secondary sources, not fetched directly. Several URLs carry primary-election IDs and need the general-election ID swapped in.
- The "live" vs. "static" labels describe how the data is delivered, not how often it updates. Many HTML lists are now frozen certified lists, because filing has closed.
- South Carolina held an extra filing period and primary. Its State Election Commission ("U.S. Senate Special Republican Party Filing & Primary," July 13, 2026, on scvotes.gov) ran them "pursuant to S.C. Code of Laws Section 7-11-55 after the death of the Republican Party nominee for U.S. Senate." Filing ran from noon July 21 to noon July 28, 2026, with the primary on Aug 11 and the runoff on Aug 25. WLTX and FITSNews name the nominee as Sen. Lindsey Graham, who died July 11, 2026. Your scraper should load both the regular and special SC candidate sets.
